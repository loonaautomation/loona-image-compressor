Why convert HEIC to JPG?

iPhones and iPads save photos as HEIC by default because the format is efficient, but HEIC still will not open in many places: online forms and portals, older apps, office software, printers and some Windows PCs either reject it or show a blank. JPG opens everywhere. Converting your iPhone photos to JPG before you share, upload or submit them removes that friction completely — the photo looks the same, it just works everywhere.

What changes in the file

HEIC stores more image per byte than JPG, using modern compression, a wider colour range and features such as multiple frames in one file. JPG is the universal standard: standard sRGB colour, 8-bit depth, and support in literally every browser, app and device. Converting therefore trades some of HEIC's efficiency for total compatibility — JPG files are often a little larger than the HEIC original at high quality, and multi-frame HEIC files produce one output image per frame here.

Worked example

An iPhone photo saved as a 1.8 MB HEIC file converts to a JPG of roughly 2.4 MB at the default 92% quality, at exactly the same pixel dimensions and the right way up. The JPG is slightly larger — that is the normal price of the format every form, portal and older app accepts — and the result line for each photo shows its real before and after sizes, so you can lower the quality if you need a smaller file.

Can I convert many photos at once?

Yes. Add as many HEIC or HEIF photos as you like — drop them in together or browse for them. They are converted one by one in a batch, and a Download all as ZIP button appears when at least one conversion succeeds.

Why might a file fail?

A file can fail if it is corrupt or uses an unsupported HEIC variant that the converter cannot decode. If a batch file fails, try converting that photo on its own, one file at a time, and check that the original still opens on your iPhone or computer.
