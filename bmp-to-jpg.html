About BMP and JPG

BMP is the old Windows bitmap format: simple, and enormous, because it stores pixels with almost no compression. Screenshots, scanner output and old photo archives still turn up as BMP, and at several megabytes each they bounce off email limits and upload forms. JPG compresses the same picture to a fraction of the size and is accepted everywhere — this page does the conversion at a quality you control.

How it works

Your BMP file is decoded locally by the browser and drawn onto a canvas at its original pixel size. If the image has transparency and the target is JPG, the canvas is first filled with your background colour, because JPG cannot store transparency. The canvas is then exported as JPG with your quality setting and the new file size is compared with the original, so the saving (or growth) you see is the real number for your file.

Worked example

A BMP screenshot from Windows at 1920 × 1080 weighs about 6.2 MB — BMP stores nearly every pixel uncompressed. Converted to JPG at 90% quality it lands near 420 KB, a saving of about 93%, ready to email or upload anywhere.

Frequently asked questions

Why are BMP files so big?

BMP stores image data with little or no compression — roughly 3 bytes per pixel — so a single full-screen screenshot is several megabytes before you do anything with it.

How much smaller will the JPG be?

Typically 90–95% smaller for screenshots and photos at 90% quality. The result line shows the exact figures for your file.

Will I see a quality difference?

For screenshots and photos at 90% quality, no visible difference in normal use. If the BMP holds fine line art you will re-edit, consider BMP to PNG instead for lossless output.

Is my image uploaded anywhere?

No. Conversion is 100% client-side in your browser.
