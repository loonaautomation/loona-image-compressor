Honest limits — please read before you start

This is a lightweight, 100% on-device converter, and it is honest about what that means:

  • Maximum clip length: 15 seconds (the range you select). Longer selections are not accepted — trim first.
  • Maximum width: 480 px and maximum frame rate: 15 fps. GIF is a heavy format; bigger or faster settings would produce enormous files and can overwhelm a phone browser.
  • Formats: MP4 and WebM only — the formats your browser can decode natively. MKV, AVI and other containers cannot be read by the browser and are not supported.
  • No audio — GIF has no sound track, so the output is silent by design.
  • If a file cannot be decoded by your browser, you will get a plain error message rather than a broken result.

How it works

Your browser decodes the video itself. The converter steps through the clip frame by frame — seeking to each exact timestamp, drawing the frame onto a canvas — and a small GIF encoder (gif.js, loaded from this same site) assembles those frames into an animated GIF. No server, no queue, no upload: everything happens on your device, which is also why the honest limits above exist.

Frequently asked questions

Is my video uploaded to a server?

No. The conversion runs entirely in your browser on your own device. Your file never leaves your device, and there is no sign-up.

Why is there a 15-second limit?

Each second of video becomes many GIF frames, and GIF files grow very fast. A 15-second cap at up to 480 px and 15 fps keeps the output a sensible size and keeps the tool reliable on phones.

Why is my GIF larger than the video?

That is normal. Video formats compress motion extremely efficiently; GIF stores every frame as an image. Shorter clips, a smaller width and a lower frame rate all make a smaller GIF.

Can I convert MKV or AVI files?

No. Browsers can only decode formats they support natively — here, MP4 and WebM. If your file is in another format, convert it to MP4 first with a desktop video editor, then use this tool.

Does the GIF keep the sound?

No. GIF is a silent image format, so the output never contains audio.
