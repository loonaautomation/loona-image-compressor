Who needs a PNG under 40 KB?

Forty KB is used by a number of Indian university and board forms and sits inside the common 20–50 KB exam band while keeping visibly better quality than the 20 KB floor.

What 40 KB does to a PNG

At 40 KB most portraits compress on quality alone, with no need to reduce dimensions — a good balance for forms that display the photo at a reasonable size.

PNG is the right choice for screenshots, logos and text images that must stay pixel-sharp — but for photographs it needs much smaller dimensions than JPG to reach the same KB.

Worked example

A typical phone photo is 3–6 MB. To reach 40 KB it must shrink by roughly 100% or more. The tool first tries to keep the full dimensions and only adjusts the encoding; only when that cannot fit 40 KB does it step the dimensions down — and it always reports the final size and dimensions, so there are no surprises when you upload the result to your form.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will my PNG be exactly 40 KB?

No — it will be at or under 40 KB, never padded up. Forms require 'at or under', so the tool searches for the best quality that fits inside the limit.

How does the PNG compressor reach 40 KB?

PNG is lossless, so there is no quality slider to turn: the only honest way to shrink a PNG is to reduce its dimensions. This tool searches dimensions until the file fits, keeping every remaining pixel exact.

Why choose PNG for a 40 KB target?

PNG is the right choice for screenshots, logos and text images that must stay pixel-sharp — but for photographs it needs much smaller dimensions than JPG to reach the same KB.

Is my image uploaded anywhere?

No. Everything runs in your browser with the Canvas API — your image never leaves your device.

What if my image cannot reach 40 KB?

The tool reduces dimensions step by step until it fits, and the result line reports the final pixel size honestly. For very detailed photos at tiny targets like 10–20 KB, a smaller result is unavoidable — portals accept this, because their own limit forces it.
