About SVG and JPG

SVG vectors are perfect for logos and illustrations, but the wider world — social platforms, office documents, printers, exam and job portals — speaks JPG. This page renders your SVG at the width you choose (1024 px by default), with proportions from its viewBox, and saves it as a JPG. Because JPG has no transparency, transparent areas are flattened onto a background colour you pick, white by default.

How it works

Your SVG file is decoded locally by the browser — for SVG, the file is parsed as text first so its viewBox and proportions are read exactly, then rendered as an image at your chosen width and drawn onto a canvas. If the image has transparency and the target is JPG, the canvas is first filled with your background colour, because JPG cannot store transparency. The canvas is then exported as JPG with your quality setting and the new file size is compared with the original, so the saving (or growth) you see is the real number for your file.

Worked example

A 24 KB SVG illustration rendered at 1024 px wide and saved as JPG at 90% quality comes out at about 180 KB, flattened onto white — ready for a social post, a document, or any form that accepts JPG but not SVG.

Frequently asked questions

What happens to transparent parts of the SVG?

JPG cannot store transparency, so transparent areas are filled with the background colour you select — white by default, which suits most documents and forms.

What size JPG will I get?

The width you choose (1024 px by default); the height follows the SVG's viewBox proportions automatically, so nothing is stretched.

SVG or JPG — which should I keep?

Keep the SVG as your master file: it scales forever. Convert to JPG copies whenever a platform, document or form needs a JPG.

Is my SVG uploaded to a server?

No — parsing and rendering happen 100% in your browser.

Is this converter free?

Yes — free, no signup, no watermark, no limit.
