About JPG, PNG and WebP

The three everyday image formats trade size against features. JPG compresses photographs very efficiently and is accepted everywhere — exam portals, email, government forms — but it has no transparency and loses a little quality every save. PNG is lossless and keeps transparency, which makes it right for logos, screenshots and text, but photo files are large. WebP, developed by Google, usually beats JPG on size at the same quality and supports transparency too, though a few older systems still cannot open it.

Converting between them is a daily need: a portal that only accepts JPG, a logo that must stay transparent in PNG, a website that wants WebP for speed. This converter handles a whole batch at once, draws each image onto a canvas at its original pixel size, flattens transparency onto your chosen background color when the target is JPG, and reports exactly how much each file shrank or grew — so you can pick a lower quality and try again if a portal has a strict KB limit.

How it works

Each image is loaded into the browser and drawn at natural size onto a canvas. The canvas is then exported in the target format with your quality setting, and the new file size is compared with the original.

Frequently asked questions

Is this image format converter free?

Yes — completely free, with no signup, no watermark and no limit on the number of images you convert.

What happens to transparency when I convert PNG to JPG?

JPEG cannot store transparency, so transparent areas are filled with the background color you pick (white by default). Converting to PNG or WebP keeps the transparency intact.

Does converting reduce image quality?

PNG output is lossless. JPG and WebP use adjustable lossy compression — at the default 90% quality the result is visually identical for photographs, and you can raise it to 100% or lower it when a file-size limit matters more.

Can I convert many images at once?

Yes. Add as many as you like; each row converts and downloads on its own, and the "Convert all & download all" button runs through the whole list one after another.

Is my image uploaded anywhere?

No. Conversion happens 100% in your browser using the Canvas API. Your images never leave your device.

Which format should I choose?

JPG for photographs and forms that need small files, PNG for logos, screenshots and anything with transparency or sharp text, and WebP for websites where the smallest sharp file wins and the audience uses modern browsers.
